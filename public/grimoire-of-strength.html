Being a Compendium of

Grimoire of Strength

A Two-Working Ritual for Mobility, Muscle & Bone-Deep Power

☽ ✦ ☾
Cast: twice weekly, thirty minutes each — with a shorter third charm optional.
Cycles: alternate Week A and Week B each week, so the workings stay fresh.
Complements: running, hiking, spin, and yoga already in your practice.
Sought: mobility, flexibility, strength, and bone-deep resilience with age.
Vessels required: yoga mat, dumbbells, resistance band, power rack.
Bound by protection: no heavy deadlifts, no heavy squats, no full sit-ups, no pull-ups — honoring an abdominal hernia.
A Word of Caution — before beginning this rite, consult a healer (physician or physical therapist) familiar with your hernia. Exhale on effort, never hold the breath, and cease any working that summons strain or pressure at the site.

First Working — Week A

Waxing Moon · Strength & Bone-Deep Power · ~28 minutes · dumbbells + rack

✦ Rite of Waking (Warm-up, 4 min)

  • Arm circles — 30 sec each direction
  • Leg swings, front-back and side-side — 10 each leg
  • Cat–Cow — 8 reps
  • Bodyweight glute bridge — 10 reps

✦ The Working Itself (~20 min)

2–3 rounds, minimal rest between exercises, 60–90 sec between rounds — keep to 2 rounds if time is short

ExerciseReps / TimeNotes
Rack-supported split squat or step-up8 / legUse the rack rail for balance, not heavy load
Dumbbell Romanian deadlift10Light–moderate weight, soft knees, hinge from the hips
Standing dumbbell overhead press8–10Builds shoulder and upper-body bone density
Barbell bench press (using the power rack)8–10Set safety pins just below chest height; lying position is low-strain for the hernia, feet flat, don't arch hard
Single-arm dumbbell row10 / armBench or rack-supported; spine neutral, no twisting
Suitcase hold30–40 sec / sideStand tall holding a heavy dumbbell at your side, shoulders level, ribs stacked over hips — resists you leaning sideways. No walking space needed.

✦ Rite of Closing (Cool-down, 5 min)

  • Standing quad stretch — 30 sec / side
  • Kneeling hip flexor stretch — 30 sec / side
  • Doorway chest & shoulder stretch — 30 sec
  • Child's pose — 45–60 sec

First Working — Week B

Waning Moon (Alternate) · Strength & Bone-Deep Power · ~28 minutes · dumbbells + rack

The deadlift and bench press remain unchanged — the rest of the working is transformed.

2–3 rounds, minimal rest between exercises, 60–90 sec between rounds

Second Working — Week A

Waxing Moon · Core, Stability & Flexibility · ~26 minutes · mat + band

✦ Rite of Waking (Warm-up, 3 min)

  • Cat–Cow — 8 reps
  • Thread the needle — 6 / side
  • Standing hip circles — 8 / side

✦ Core & Stability Circuit (~13 min)

2–3 rounds

ExerciseReps / TimeNotes
Bird dog10 / sideSlow and controlled, no low-back arch
Dead bug10 / sideLow back stays flat on the mat
Side plank20–30 sec / sideKnees down if needed; no breath-holding
Band Pallof press10 / side"Press and resist" — anchor the band at chest height, press straight out and hold, then return. The point is not letting your torso twist toward the anchor.
Glute bridge (or banded hip thrust)12Squeeze glutes, not the lower back

✦ Mobility Flow (5 min)

  • 90/90 hip switches — 6 / side — sit with front leg bent 90° in front, back leg bent 90° to the side, rotate knees side to side keeping the chest tall
  • Thoracic rotations (quadruped) — 8 / side — hands and knees, one hand behind the head, rotate the elbow up toward the ceiling then down under the torso
  • World's Greatest Stretch — 5 / side — from a lunge, drop the back hand to the floor, then rotate that same arm up toward the ceiling

✦ Rite of Closing (Cool-down, 5 min)

  • Figure-4 stretch (lying) — 30 sec / side
  • Gentle supine spinal twist — 30 sec / side
  • Pigeon pose or seated glute stretch — 30 sec / side
  • Deep breathing in child's pose — 45 sec

Second Working — Week B

Waning Moon (Alternate) · Core, Stability & Flexibility · ~26 minutes · mat + band

✦ Rite of Waking (Warm-up, 3 min)

  • Thread the needle — 6 / side
  • Cat–Cow — 8 reps
  • Standing marches with arm reach — 10 / side

✦ Core & Stability Circuit (~13 min)

2–3 rounds

ExerciseReps / TimeNotes
Half-kneeling Pallof press10 / sideSame "press and resist" cue as the standing version, from a half-kneeling position — challenges hip and core stability together
Bear crawl hold20–30 secHands and toes on the mat, knees hovering just off the floor, flat back — no breath-holding
Forearm plank with shoulder taps10 taps / sideFeet a bit wider for stability; keep hips still as you tap
Single-leg glute bridge8–10 / sideKeep hips level, don't let them dip
Prone superman hold (gentle)15–20 secLift arms and legs slightly off the mat, low intensity — light posterior-chain work, not a hard arch

✦ Mobility Flow (5 min)

  • Windmill stretch — 6 / side — stand wide, hinge slightly forward, rotate reaching one hand to the sky while the other reaches down
  • Standing figure-4 to hip hinge — 5 / side — cross ankle over opposite knee, hinge forward gently, stand back up
  • Cobra pose (gentle) — 3 breaths, held lightly

✦ Rite of Closing (Cool-down, 5 min)

  • Band-assisted supine hamstring stretch — 30 sec / side
  • Seated gentle spinal twist — 30 sec / side
  • Legs-up-the-wall — 90 sec (great for recovery and circulation)
  • Deep breathing in child's pose — 45 sec

The Quick Charm

Optional Mobility & Flexibility Reset · 10–15 minutes

Cast on a rest day, or split from the Second Working for a more frequent mobility habit.

  • Cat–Cow — 10 reps
  • World's Greatest Stretch — 5 / side
  • 90/90 hip switches — 8 / side
  • Thoracic rotations — 8 / side
  • Standing forward fold (soft knees, don't force it) — 30 sec
  • Pigeon pose — 45 sec / side
  • Box breathing or slow nasal breathing — 1 min

✦ On the Growth of Power ✦

  • Add weight only when every set is completed with good form and no strain at the hernia site.
  • Every 3–4 weeks, add one rep per set, or a small weight increase (2.5–5 lb), rather than jumping sets and reps at once.
  • The holds, presses, and deadlifts are your chief drivers of bone density here — favor consistency over heavy load.
  • If any movement ever feels wrong for your hernia, exchange it for a bodyweight or banded form rather than pushing through.
